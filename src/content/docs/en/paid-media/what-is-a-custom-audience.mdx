A custom audience is, in essence, showing an ad to a real person instead of to a description of someone. That difference, small as it looks, changes how much each ad costs you and who you can reach afterwards.

## What a custom audience actually is

When you upload a list of real contacts (for example, the people who already bought a ticket to your event) to Meta, Google or TikTok, that platform turns it into an audience you can target ads to. Meta and TikTok call this a **Custom Audience**; Google calls it **Customer Match**. The name changes, the idea is the same.

In Nevent, that starting point is a segment: the group of fans you've already defined with your own criteria, such as attendees of a specific event or buyers of a particular add-on.

## Why advertising to people who already know you is cheaper

An ad platform doesn't charge the same for every impression. It charges more to find the right person when it doesn't know who that is, and less when it already does.

When you target by interests or demographics, you're asking the platform to guess: "find someone 25 to 40 years old who likes music festivals in this city". That's a description, not a certainty, and building an audience from a description costs more.

When you give it a custom audience, there's nothing to guess: these are people who already bought. Explaining who you are to someone who already knows you costs nothing, because there's no need to explain it. In practice, that saving translates into a lower cost per result.

## Where real growth comes from: lookalike audiences

The most valuable part of a custom audience isn't just advertising again to people who already bought. It's what the platform can do with that sample: find new people who resemble your real buyers, in behavior and characteristics, even if they've never heard of your event.

This is called a **lookalike audience**. The more real data you give the platform about who actually buys, the better its judgment becomes at finding more people like them. It's the most cost-effective growth path there is in digital advertising today: you're not guessing who to advertise to, you're letting the algorithm learn from people who already showed real interest.

## Showing your audience versus describing your audience

The underlying difference between a custom audience and interest-based targeting is this:

**Targeting by interests is describing your audience.** You tell the platform what you think the people you're interested in like, using the categories that platform makes available to you. It's an approximation, built from the outside.

**A custom audience is showing your audience.** You give the platform data about real people who already interacted with you. There's no assumption: there's evidence.

Neither approach is useless. Targeting by interests still makes sense when you don't have your own buyers yet, for example when launching a brand-new event for the first time. But once you have a sales history, a custom audience and its lookalike audiences tend to perform better, because they start from real data instead of guesswork.

## What this feature doesn't solve yet

A custom audience helps you decide who sees your ad. It doesn't, today, let you precisely measure how much each sale generated by that ad costs you.

Nevent doesn't send the sales that happen in your ticketing system back to Meta, Google or TikTok. That means that even if you sync your audience and run campaigns targeting it, the platform won't know which specific purchases came from that ad. Use this feature to target better and to generate lookalike audiences, not as a source for your real acquisition cost.
