## The problem Paid Media solves

Organizing advertising for an event almost always means working with more than one platform. A promoter might run awareness campaigns on Meta, traffic campaigns on TikTok and search campaigns on Google, all at the same time, for the same event.

The usual result is that information ends up scattered: Meta spend in Meta Ads Manager, TikTok spend in TikTok Ads Manager, Google spend in Google Ads. Comparing performance across platforms means opening three different tools, exporting data or building your own spreadsheets.

Paid Media in Nevent exists to solve exactly that: centralizing the data from every connected advertising platform in a single panel, with common filters and comparable metrics.

## What the module does and doesn't do

It's important to keep these two levels separate:

**What the module doesn't do.** It doesn't create ads or campaigns. Ads are still created and managed in Meta Ads Manager, Google Ads or TikTok Ads Manager, exactly as before. Nevent doesn't interfere with that process.

**What the module does do.** It connects to your authorized ad accounts and brings performance data into a unified screen inside Nevent. From there, the promoter can see total spend, results and cost per result across all their campaigns, filter them by platform, period or status, and check each campaign's detail without leaving Nevent.

## What the unified view brings to the promoter

### Comparing platforms with the same criteria

Each advertising platform presents its data with its own terminology and its own format. Nevent normalizes that information and shows it with the same columns across every platform: spend, impressions, reach, clicks, CTR, results and cost per result. That lets you compare, for example, whether Meta or Google is giving a lower cost per result for a specific event, without having to translate metrics between systems.

### Managing budget from a single place

The panel shows accumulated spend for the selected period (7 days, 14 days, 30 days or a custom range) across all active campaigns. With that aggregated figure, the promoter can see at a glance how much has been invested and where the budget is going, without opening each platform separately.

### Filtering by status to focus on what matters

Campaigns have three possible states: active, paused or archived. The status filter lets you isolate only the campaigns running right now, without the noise of old or stopped campaigns. That's useful during an event's on-sale weeks, when it pays to closely watch the performance of what's currently active.

### Accessing detail when needed

The campaign panel is the summary view. When a result stands out, whether it's higher spend than expected or a particularly good CTR, the promoter can drill into that campaign's detail to see which ad sets make it up and how each one is performing.

## How the data is organized

The module has one main screen with two clearly separated parts.

The header shows four global indicators for the selected period and platform: total spend, number of results, cost per result and ROAS (return on ad spend). These numbers change every time you adjust the filters.

Below the header is the campaign table. Each row is a campaign, with its source platform, status, objective and metrics for the period. The columns are configurable, so the promoter can show or hide whichever ones are most relevant to their case.

The table also indicates how many campaigns have active data in the selected period, which helps explain why some rows show dashes: a campaign that was paused during those days has no data to show for that period.

## Why the time period matters

The choice of period affects every piece of data you see on the panel: both the header indicators and each campaign's metrics in the table. A 7-day period shows recent performance; a wider period, like 30 days or the full history, lets you see trends and compare different phases of the campaign.

The panel always shows when the last sync with the platforms happened, so you know roughly how fresh the data you're looking at is.

## Relationship with the rest of Nevent

Paid Media is part of Nevent's set of analytics tools, alongside sales analytics and email campaign analytics. The underlying idea is the same in every case: reduce the number of external tools a promoter needs to check to understand how an event is going.

Paid advertising data isn't isolated from the rest of the tool.
