When you connect your ticketing platform to Nevent, you stop relying on file uploads to keep your audience current. That difference, small as it seems, changes what you can do with your campaigns, your ads, your chatbot and your sales reports.

## Two ways to get your fans into Nevent

There are two paths for your fans' data to reach Nevent.

The first is **importing a file**: you upload a CSV or Excel with a list of contacts, as explained in the guide [Import your contacts](/en/audience/import-your-contacts/). It's fast and you don't need anything besides the file.

The second is **connecting your ticketing platform**: you give Nevent your ticketing provider's credentials (Fourvenues, Enterticket, DICE, Onebox and others) and, from then on, every sale and every attendee enters your audience automatically, as explained in the guide [How to connect your ticketing platform](/en/audience/connect-your-ticketing-platform/).

Both paths are valid. The important difference is in what happens after day one.

## The real difference: live data versus frozen data

An imported file is a snapshot. It reflects who bought up to the exact moment you generated that CSV. From then on, that list doesn't change on its own: if you sell 200 more tickets tomorrow, those 200 fans won't appear in Nevent until you export, map and upload a new file again.

A connected ticketing platform, on the other hand, keeps the flow open. Every new purchase, every attendee who checks in to an event, is reflected in your Nevent audience without you having to do anything. Your data is always live, never ageing while it waits for the next manual upload.

This difference isn't just technical. It translates into very concrete things you can or can't do, depending on which path you chose.

## What a connected source lets you do that a CSV can't

### Segments that regenerate themselves for your campaigns

A segment in Nevent (like the one you learn to create in [your first segment](/en/segmentation/your-first-segment/)) automatically recalculates who meets its conditions every time you launch a campaign.

If your ticketing platform is connected, that recalculation includes fans who bought yesterday, this morning or five minutes ago. If you only have a CSV imported three months ago, the segment will stay faithful to that date: nobody who bought afterwards will show up, no matter how much the segment's condition says "bought a ticket this month".

### Ad audiences that grow without you doing anything

When you use your fans to build ad audiences on Meta, Google or TikTok, the value of that audience depends on it being up to date. A connected audience grows every week with real, recent buyers, which improves the quality of the audience you show your ads to.

An audience based on an old CSV, on the other hand, stays fixed: it doesn't add your most recent buyers, and it doesn't stop including people who are no longer relevant.

### Real-time context for the chatbot

Nevent's chatbot responds to your fans using the information it has on them: what tickets they hold, what they bought, which events they attended. If that information comes from a connected ticketing platform, the chatbot can confirm a recent purchase, give details about last week's event, or resolve a question with current data.

With an imported CSV, the chatbot only knows what was in that file on the day you uploaded it. A purchase from yesterday isn't part of what the chatbot can see or discuss.

### Sales traceability: knowing which campaign generated which sale

When your ticketing platform is connected, Nevent can link a specific sale to the campaign, segment or ad that originated it. That lets you know, for example, how many tickets your last email campaign sold, or which Meta ad brought in the most buyers.

An imported file doesn't have that thread: it comes in as a list of contacts, with no information about which marketing action led them to buy.

## When it still makes sense to import

Importing a CSV is still useful in several cases: when you don't yet have a ticketing platform connected, when you want to upload a one-off list of contacts that don't come from a sale (like the attendees of an old event), or while you're setting up the connection with your provider.

It's not a mistake to use it. It's simply a data source with less reach than a direct connection. When you can, connecting your ticketing platform gives you everything a CSV does and, on top of that, keeps everything up to date without any effort on your part.
