The **Events** tab on a fan's record gathers their whole history with your organisation: which events they attended, how many tickets they bought and how much they spent. This guide shows you how to read it. About 4 minutes.

## Before you start

You need a fan with at least one recorded purchase. If you have not connected your ticketing platform yet, follow [Connect your ticketing platform](/en/audience/connect-your-ticketing-platform) first: without synced sales the history is empty.

## Step 1: Open the Events tab on the fan record

In the side menu, click **Fans** and search for the fan by name or email. Click their row to open the record.

In the record menu, go to **Events**.

![Events tab on fan Aitana Demo's record, with the Tickets bought, Ticket value and Recorded purchases cards, the incomplete-data notice and the table with one event](/images/guias/audiencia/eventos-de-un-fan/paso-1-pestana-eventos.png)

## Step 2: Interpret the three history figures

The three cards at the top summarise the fan's entire relationship with you, not just the visible period:

| Figure | What it means |
|---|---|
| Tickets bought | Tickets the fan purchased, with invitations counted separately |
| Ticket value | Sum of reported historical values; it may exclude fees, so it is not the total paid |
| Recorded purchases | Settled gross plus certified cashless spend |

When a figure cannot be calculated you will see **No data** or **Not applicable** instead of a zero. They mean different things: "No data" is missing information, "Not applicable" is information that does not exist for that fan.

:::note[Why the incomplete-data notice appears]
If the history has gaps, a notice appears above the table saying some history data is incomplete. It is not an error: the detail panel for each event explains the specific reason.
:::

## Step 3: Open an event's detail panel

Click any row in the table. A side panel opens with that event's figures and up to three tabs:

- **Tickets**: the related orders, with their date, channel and amount.
- **Marketing**: the campaigns linked to that event. If there are none, the panel says so.
- **Details**: the data's traceability.

![Side panel for DEMO FEST 2026 with the tickets, value, add-ons and recorded figures, and the Tickets tab open showing the order and its amount](/images/guias/audiencia/eventos-de-un-fan/paso-2-panel-detalle-entradas.png)

## Step 4: Check data quality in Details

The **Details** tab explains why a number is what it is. It gathers three blocks:

- **Data quality**: the specific reasons why information is missing, for example that it is unknown whether some tickets were bought or given away.
- **What this row groups**: if the row brings together several editions of the same event, you see which ones here.
- **Updated**: the date and time of the last update for amounts, tickets, groupings and marketing.

![Details tab of the panel with the Data quality, What this row groups and Updated blocks showing the timestamps for amounts, tickets, groupings and marketing](/images/guias/audiencia/eventos-de-un-fan/paso-3-panel-detalles-calidad.png)

## Result

You can now answer, for any fan, which events they attended and how much they spent, and you know where to look when a figure does not add up. Use it before segmenting: a fan with an incomplete history may fall out of a spend-based segment through no fault of their own.
