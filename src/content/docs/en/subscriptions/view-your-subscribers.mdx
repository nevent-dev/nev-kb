Knowing who is subscribed to your communications is something you will check often: to confirm a form is collecting sign-ups, to answer someone who asks why they get your emails, or to prepare a send to the people who signed up through a specific channel.

Nevent shows it to you from two angles. You can start from a subscription and see its whole list, or start from a person and see which subscriptions they belong to. In this guide you go through both, and you also set up the form so that it classifies each new subscriber on its own. About 8 minutes.

## Before you start

You need at least one subscription created. If you do not have one yet, first follow the guide [create your first subscription form](/en/subscriptions/create-a-subscription-form/).

## Step 1: Open a subscription's subscriber list

In the left side menu, in the **Audience** block, click **Subscriptions**.

You will see the list of all your forms, with each one's name, its status and the number of subscribers it has collected. That counter already gives you a first reading of which one is working.

![The "All subscriptions" list in DEMO FEST, with the "BUSCANDO MOSCAS" subscription Active, 5,872 subscribers and its creation date](/images/guias/suscripciones/consulta-tus-suscriptores/paso-1-listado-suscripciones.png)

Click the form you want to check. Nevent opens its management panel.

In that panel's side menu, click **Subscribers**.

![The "Subscribers" section of the "BUSCANDO MOSCAS" subscription, with the search box, the date range filter and the subscribers table with its Email, Name, Status and Subscription date columns](/images/guias/suscripciones/consulta-tus-suscriptores/paso-2-lista-suscriptores.png)

## Step 2: Read the list

Each row of the list is a person who signed up to that subscription. These are its four columns:

| Column | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| Email | The email address they subscribed with. It is the minimum the form always asks for. |
| Name | The person's name, if your form asked for it. If it did not, it is empty. |
| Status | Whether they are still subscribed (**Confirmed**) or have unsubscribed (**Opted out**). |
| Subscription date | The exact day and time they signed up. |

You can sort all four columns by clicking their header. **Subscription date** is more useful than it looks: sorting by it you see at a glance whether your form is still collecting people or has been idle for weeks, and you can relate sign-up peaks to the actions you launched on those days.

Above the table you have two ways to narrow the list, which is what you will really use once you have thousands of subscribers:

- **The search box**, which filters by email or by name. It is for quickly checking whether a specific person is on this list.
- **The date range**, which keeps only the sign-ups between two days. With it you can answer questions such as how many people came in the week you launched the presale campaign.

The clear button removes both filters at once and brings back the full list.

:::note[About the Opted out status]
A person becomes **Opted out** when they click the unsubscribe link included in any of your campaigns. Nevent handles it on its own: it stops including them in your marketing sends from that moment.

Do not delete that person from your database. Their historical data is still useful and the opt-out is already respected.
:::

## Step 3: Check a specific fan's subscriptions

Sometimes the question goes the other way. You do not want a subscription's list; you want to know what is going on with one particular person.

In the side menu, in the **Fans** block, click **Fans**.

Find the person by their name or email in the search box at the top of the table, and click their row to open their profile.

In the profile menu, click **Subscriptions**.

![The Subscriptions section of the fan Abril Aguilar Aguilar, with one row for the "BUSCANDO MOSCAS" subscription, Confirmed, dated 11/08/2025, source Widget and active Yes](/images/guias/suscripciones/consulta-tus-suscriptores/paso-3-suscripciones-del-fan.png)

Here you see all the subscriptions in your organization that this person belongs to, one per row:

| Column | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| Subscription | The name of the form they signed up to, with its description below. |
| Status | Whether it is still **Confirmed** or **Opted out**. |
| Subscription date | The day and time they signed up. |
| Source | How they came in. **Widget** means they filled in the form embedded on a website. |
| Active | Whether that subscription is still active in your organization. |

The **All statuses** dropdown, above the table, lets you keep only the confirmed ones or only the opt-outs. It is useful for fans who have been with you for years and have several subscriptions.

This is the view you need when someone writes to you asking why they get, or do not get, your emails. In ten seconds you know whether they are subscribed, to what, since when and how they came in.

:::tip[If the table is empty]
A fan having no subscriptions is not an error. It means they reached your database another way, usually by buying a ticket or through a contact import, and never filled in a subscription form.

Being in your database and being subscribed are two different things, and the difference has legal consequences. We explain it in [what subscriptions are for](/en/subscriptions/what-subscriptions-are-for/).
:::

## Step 4: Get new subscribers classified on their own

Checking the list is fine, but what really saves work is having each person come in already labeled. You set that up only once.

Go back to **Audience > Subscriptions**, open your form and click **Automations**.

![The "Automations" section of DEMO FEST with its two subsections: "Automatic Folder Assignment" with the folder selector, and "Automatic Custom Field Assignment" with the "Create" button](/images/guias/suscripciones/crear-formulario/paso-8-automatizaciones.png)

There are two things you can automate:

- **Automatic folder assignment.** Choose one or more folders of your organization. Everyone who subscribes through this form is assigned to them right away. For example, a folder called "Web 2026".
- **Automatic custom field assignment.** If your organization has custom fields, you can give them a fixed value for everyone who comes in through here. For example, a "Source" field with the value "Web form".

When you have chosen, click **Save**.

From then on, each new subscriber arrives with that information filled in. Those who were already subscribed are not changed.

## Step 5: Use that classification to segment

This is where the work of the previous step pays you back.

Custom fields are a direct segmentation criterion: you find them in the **Fan attributes** category when you build a segment. So you can create, for example, a segment of everyone whose "Source" field is "Web form" and send them a different campaign from the rest of your database.

With folders you can filter both when creating a segment and when choosing a campaign's audience.

The practical result is that you can treat differently those who signed up on your website, those who did so at an in-person activation and those who came through a specific promotion, without ever having to separate them by hand.

If you have never created a segment, start with [create your first segment](/en/segmentation/your-first-segment/).

## Result

After this guide you know:

- Where the full subscriber list of any of your forms is, and what each column means.
- How to check, from a person's record, which subscriptions they belong to and since when.
- How to set up the form so that it classifies each new subscriber on its own with a folder and a custom field.
- How to use that classification to segment and send different campaigns depending on where each fan came from.
