<Aside type="tip">
**Who this playbook is for:** promoters with at least one previous event in Nevent and a fan base with purchase history. If you're just getting started and don't have prior sales data yet, save this playbook for your second launch.
</Aside>

You launch a presale and the first thing you want is for the first phase to sell out fast. An early sold-out creates social pressure, generates organic buzz, and validates the event before you have to spend on advertising. The problem is that most promoters open the sale to their entire base at once, which dilutes the message and wastes the strongest signal they have: the fans who've already proven they buy fast.

This playbook shows you how to use that history to kick off the presale in an orderly way, in two waves, using the segmentation and campaign tools you already have in Nevent.

---

## When to use this playbook

Use this playbook whenever you launch a first sales phase with a price or terms different from later phases: early bird, discounted presale, early access with limited capacity, or any product where scarcity is part of the value.

Don't use it if:

- Your sales history is less than a year old, or doesn't include presale phases.
- The new event is a very different genre or format from your previous events (early history doesn't predict behavior well in that case).
- You're going to open the sale to your entire base at once: if there's no exclusive window, there's no reason to segment by early behavior.

---

## Playbook overview

The playbook runs in three phases:

1. **Preparation** (one or two days before launch): you build the two key segments and prepare the campaigns.
2. **Wave 1** (24-48 hours before the public opening): you message only early fans and your Champions.
3. **Wave 2** (public opening): you message the rest of your base once the first phase has already been running for a few hours.

---

## Phase 1: Prepare the segments

### Main segment: early fans

The core of this playbook is the segment of fans who bought in the first hours or in early-bird phases at previous events. These fans are trained to act fast — they don't need convincing, just a heads-up.

To build this segment, follow the steps in the [early-bird fans guide](/en/segmentation/use-cases/early-buyers/). The basic logic is:

- Fans who've bought early-bird, presale or first-phase tickets at any of your previous events.
- Limit the time window to the last two or three years so you don't include fans who bought a long time ago and are no longer active.
- If the event is local and doesn't justify travel, add a city or region filter.
- Exclude anyone who already has a ticket for the current event, if the sale has already started through some channel.

The ticket-type criterion is what underpins this segment. You can dig deeper into how to use it in the [spend and consumption reference](/en/segmentation/capabilities/spend-and-consumption/), which explains that the ticket type chosen reflects not just spending power but also purchase behavior.

For the mechanical steps of creating the segment in Nevent, see [how to create a segment](/en/segmentation/segmentation-engine/creating-a-segment/).

### Complementary segment: RFM Champions

If you want to give even more exclusive treatment to your highest-value group, add a second segment with fans classified as Champions in Nevent's automatic RFM model. Champions are fans with high recency, high frequency and high spend: they make up between 1% and 3% of a typical base but account for a disproportionate share of revenue.

The idea is to message them even before the rest of your early fans, or with even earlier access, or with a differentiated offer (for example, a VIP ticket or upgrade). The full case is in the [VIP segment with RFM Champions guide](/en/segmentation/use-cases/top-rfm-vip/).

You can understand how the model works and the 11 available profiles in the [automatic RFM reference](/en/segmentation/capabilities/rfm/).

<Aside type="note">
If your first phase's capacity is small (fewer than 200 tickets), consider messaging only Champions first and saving the early-fans message for a second window a few hours later. This maximizes the sense of exclusivity and shortens the time to sold-out.
</Aside>

---

## Phase 2: Prepare the campaigns

With the segments created, prepare the campaigns before launch. You'll need at least two: one for the exclusive wave (early fans and Champions) and one for the general opening.

To create each campaign, follow the [how to create your first campaign](/en/campaigns/create-your-first-campaign/) wizard. The process has 8 steps and takes less than 10 minutes to complete.

### Wave 1 campaign (early fans and Champions)

**Recommended channel:** email. It's the main channel for early-access communication because it allows context and has good open and click tracking. If you have WhatsApp enabled, you can prepare a short second message for the same day as launch that reinforces the notice (see [how to create WhatsApp templates](/en/campaigns/whatsapp-templates/) if you don't have one set up yet).

**Subject line:** be direct and mention exclusivity. Examples that work:

- "Early access to [event name] tickets, just for you"
- "[Event name] Early Bird: 24 hours before anyone else"
- "Your early access to [event name] is here"

The [best practices for your campaigns guide](/en/campaigns/best-practices/) reminds you that the subject line is the biggest factor in open rate. Make sure it's clear, specific, and that the benefit to the fan shows up in the first 40 characters (what's visible on mobile).

**Message body:** explain in two or three lines why this fan is getting the notice before anyone else (because they were among the first in the past), that tickets are limited, and that preferred access expires within a specific window. Include a single call-to-action button linking directly to the purchase page.

**Scheduling:** schedule the send between 24 and 48 hours before the public opening. That gap creates real urgency without pushing too hard.

**Audience:** select the early-fans segment in step 5 of the wizard. If you've also created the Champions segment as a separate group, you can select both in the same step (Nevent lets you combine several segments in one campaign).

### Wave 2 campaign (public opening)

This campaign goes to the rest of your base — the general public who aren't part of the early-fans segment. You can launch it once the first phase has already been selling for a few hours, so the public-opening message naturally incorporates the fact that tickets are already in circulation.

If the first phase is nearly sold out by that point, mention it in the subject line: "[Event name]'s Early Bird tickets are flying" activates scarcity-driven urgency in the general public better than any other formula.

---

## Phase 3: Launch and measure

### Launch

On the day of the exclusive notice, launch the Wave 1 campaign at the scheduled time. Check from **Campaigns - My campaigns** that the status moves to "Sending" and then "Sent" without errors. To understand statuses and available actions, see the guide [managing your campaigns](/en/campaigns/manage-your-campaigns/).

If you had a WhatsApp message ready, launch it to the same audience a few hours after the email, once it's the day of the official launch.

### Metrics to check

A few hours after the send, open the campaign report. The key indicators are:

**Open rate:** the benchmark for well-maintained early-fans campaigns is between 35% and 55%. If your campaign is above 25%, that's already a healthy sign. Below 15%, review the subject line and sender name. The guide [how to interpret your campaign results](/en/campaigns/report/results/) explains what each number means and what to do if they're low.

**Click rate:** a healthy result is above 5% of sent. If it's low, check that the email has a single, clear call-to-action button and that the link goes to the correct purchase page.

**Speed of first-phase sellout:** this is the playbook's definitive indicator. If the early-bird phase fills up before you launch Wave 2, the playbook worked. If it doesn't fill up but drops below 20% availability in the first hours, that's also a very good result.

To see exactly who opened and clicked (and use that list for future campaigns), see [who opened and clicked your campaign](/en/campaigns/report/opens-and-clicks/).

---

## How to measure the overall result

Compare these three numbers between Wave 1 and Wave 2:

| Indicator | Wave 1 (early fans) | Wave 2 (general public) |
|---|---|---|
| Open rate | Target: above 35% | Healthy benchmark: above 25% |
| Click rate | Target: above 8% | Healthy benchmark: above 5% |
| Purchase speed | Hours to sold-out or 80% | Days to reach that same level |

If Wave 1 clearly beats Wave 2 on opens and clicks, you've confirmed that the early-fans segment is a high-intent audience. Save that segment with a descriptive name to reuse it for future launches.

---

## Common mistakes

**Sending to your entire base at once.** This is the most common mistake. If you don't create an exclusive window for early fans, there's no difference between messaging fast buyers and everyone else. The value of this segment only kicks in if they get the notice before everyone else.

**Giving too much time to the exclusive window.** More than 72 hours of early access dilutes the urgency. The fan knows they have time and puts it off. A 24-to-48-hour window is enough to create pressure without the offer expiring before active fans see it.

**Not mentioning why this fan is getting the notice.** The message works better when the fan understands you're recognizing their past behavior. A line like "You're among the first to know because you were one of the first to buy for [previous event name]" turns a generic email into a message the fan perceives as personal.

**Using the segment for an event of a very different genre.** If the early-bird history comes from electronic music festivals and the new event is flamenco, the segment may not predict behavior well. In that case, lean more on the RFM Champions segment, which is genre-independent.

**Not reviewing the metrics before launching Wave 2.** Use the open and click data from Wave 1 to adjust the subject line and body of Wave 2. That information is available a few hours after the send.
