This guide explains what a bounce is, how to see them in the report of a sent campaign, and why it's worth reviewing them. About 3 minutes.

## Before you start

You need at least one campaign in **Sent** status. Follow [How to create your first campaign](/en/campaigns/create-your-first-campaign/) first if you haven't launched one yet.

## Step 1: Open the Deliverability section

In the side menu, click **My campaigns**, then the name of a sent campaign.

Inside the report, click **Deliverability** in the left-hand menu.

## Step 2: Read the deliverability data

This section shows five cards with the send's delivery data.

The data you'll see is:

- **Sent:** emails Nevent attempted to deliver.
- **Delivered:** the ones the recipient's server accepted. A "delivered" email is not a guarantee it reached the inbox: it may have ended up in the spam folder.
- **Delivery rate:** the percentage of sent emails that arrived correctly.
- **Soft bounces:** failed deliveries due to a temporary problem, such as a full inbox or a server momentarily down. Nevent may retry the send.
- **Hard bounces:** failed deliveries due to a permanent problem, such as an email address that doesn't exist. Nevent automatically flags these addresses so it doesn't send to them again.

## Why bounces matter

Every hard bounce you accumulate damages your reputation as a sender. If your hard bounce percentage rises above 2%, email servers start treating you as an unreliable source, making it harder for your future campaigns to get through, even to valid contacts.

The best way to keep bounces low is to keep your list clean: regularly remove contacts who've had several undelivered sends in a row.

## What to do if you have hard bounces

If you spot hard bounces in the report:

1. Click **View** next to "Hard bounces" to see the list of addresses.
2. Check whether they're incorrect addresses or domains that no longer exist.
3. Nevent already excludes them automatically from future sends, but it's good practice to also clean up your contact database.

:::tip[Tip]
A delivery rate above 95% is healthy. If it drops below 90%, review the quality of your contact list.
:::
